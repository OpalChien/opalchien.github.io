A Mask's Position Matters as Much as Its Accuracy

Track spacing, orientation, and coordinate space from DICOM to NIfTI so segmentation stays aligned through conversion.

DICOMNIfTISegmentation

Sample article · Written to demonstrate the reading layout; not an original publication by the author.

Matching array shapes do not guarantee that an image and its mask share a physical space. Three-dimensional imaging requires tracking spacing, origin, direction, and every transformation applied during preprocessing.

Distinguish indices from physical coordinates

Array indices describe data organization in memory, while physical coordinates describe position in space. Imaging libraries may follow different axis orders and coordinate conventions. Confirm the assumptions of both the reader and writer before conversion.

  • Record shape, spacing, origin, and the direction matrix on input.
  • Keep reversible transformation information for every crop, resampling, or reorientation.
  • Transform predictions into the intended reference image space before export.
  • Inspect multiplanar overlays for laterality, position, and boundary alignment.

Choose interpolation for the data type

Discrete label masks usually use nearest-neighbor interpolation to avoid introducing nonexistent labels. Images and probability maps need interpolation suited to their purpose. A shared target shape is not enough; the target coordinate space must also match.

Check alignment using both metadata and overlays. Either alone can miss axis-order or orientation mistakes.

Make exported results reviewable

For each case, save a source metadata summary, preprocessing settings, the final mask, and representative overlays. Use the voxel volume in the actual output space for volume calculations, and state the units and method in the results table.
