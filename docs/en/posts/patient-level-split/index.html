Split Patients Before Images: The Basics of Data Partitioning

Examinations from the same patient may be more similar than expected. Establish independence before training.

Data LeakageValidation

Sample article · Written to demonstrate the reading layout; not an original publication by the author.

An imaging dataset is not necessarily a collection of independent samples. A patient may have multiple examinations, an examination may contain different reconstructions, and a series may produce many slices. Shuffling all images can obscure those relationships.

Start with a traceable data manifest

Map every image to its patient, study, and series, then choose the grouping level that fits the research question. For patient-level prediction, group by patient ID before partitioning. Separately inspect repeated exports, derived images, and near-duplicate copies.

Save the split as a research artifact

  • Keep split.csv with anonymized IDs, partitions, and folds.
  • Fix the seed and save the actual assignments.
  • Describe patient counts, examination counts, and label distributions for each partition.
  • Ensure normalization, feature selection, and threshold setting use only the permitted training or validation data.

If the question concerns generalization across time, hospitals, or devices, the validation design needs corresponding independent partitions. Non-overlapping patients are a starting point; they do not replace the external validation required by the research question.
